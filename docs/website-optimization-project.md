# Website Optimization Project — HR Service Selection Record

**Site:** Human Resources Outsourced
**Decision date:** 2026-09-15
**Scope:** Current remediation decision record; this document does not claim to reconstruct or prove any earlier Paperclip or optimization run.

## Service selection method

The current Website Optimization Project reviewed the site's existing service taxonomy against four publication tests:

1. **Industry fit:** the work is a recurring administrative burden in recruiting or people operations.
2. **Virtual assistant feasibility:** a Philippines-based support professional can prepare, coordinate, check, document, or route the work through client-approved systems.
3. **Decision boundary:** the client retains employment decisions, policy interpretation, pay and benefits authority, legal conclusions, sensitive communications, and approval of consequential changes.
4. **Reviewability:** the service can start as a narrow queue with a named owner, limited access, written stop rules, and evidence for closeout.

This is a service-design decision, not a claim that every employer should outsource every listed task. Scope, access, applicable law, collective arrangements, security requirements, and internal expertise must be assessed by each client.

## Selected service architecture

| Route | Why it is offered | Realistic VA contribution | Employer/client retains |
|---|---|---|---|
| `operations-support` | HR inboxes, record changes, acknowledgements, and follow-ups create recurring coordination work. | Sort approved requests, maintain checklists, prepare routine replies, and route exceptions. | Policy, employee-relations, pay, discipline, investigation, and legal decisions. |
| `customer-support` | Employees need consistent routing and status updates for routine HR questions. | Classify requests, use approved knowledge, request missing fields, and maintain status. | Sensitive answers, eligibility, disputes, accommodations, and policy interpretation. |
| `admin-support` | Recruiting pipelines often stall on calendars, reminders, and record upkeep. | Schedule interviews, send approved logistics, collect scorecards, and update factual status. | Selection, rejection, compensation, offer terms, and candidate assessment. |
| `reporting-and-qa` | HR teams need repeatable checks of queues, paperwork, and report definitions. | Prepare source-linked reports, reconcile records, flag exceptions, and maintain evidence. | Metric interpretation, risk decisions, external publication, and corrective action. |
| `candidate-sourcing` | Approved requisitions require time-consuming research and longlist administration. | Research permitted sources, maintain factual candidate records, and deduplicate entries. | Criteria approval, shortlist decisions, protected-trait judgments, and selection. |
| `interview-coordination` | Multi-party interviews create time-zone, panel, communication, and scorecard dependencies. | Coordinate availability, approved messages, reminders, and calendar updates. | Interview judgment, accommodations, disposition, offers, and sensitive feedback. |
| `employee-records-administration` | Employee changes and files require controlled intake, filing, and reconciliation. | Check required fields, prepare approved updates, file records, and retain change evidence. | Authoritative values, identity conflicts, retention policy, and sensitive-record decisions. |
| `benefits-administration-support` | Enrollment packets and carrier handoffs produce deadline and completeness queues. | Check administrative completeness, track transmission status, and route exceptions. | Eligibility, plan advice, disputed coverage, legal interpretation, and final approval. |
| `payroll-preparation-support` | Payroll inputs arrive from multiple systems and owners before cutoff. | Assemble approved inputs, compare values, record variances, and prepare review registers. | Estimation, tax treatment, deductions, corrections, submission, and payment approval. |
| `onboarding-coordination` | New hires depend on synchronized HR, manager, IT, payroll, and equipment tasks. | Open approved checklists, chase owners, coordinate logistics, and record completion. | Work authorization, access scope, employment terms, and exception decisions. |
| `learning-administration` | Training programs require roster, version, reminder, and completion administration. | Load approved audiences, send reminders, reconcile evidence, and report exceptions. | Legal training requirements, exemptions, content approval, and disputed completion. |
| `hr-help-desk-support` | Shared HR queues benefit from consistent intake and fast routing. | Categorize requests, answer from approved knowledge, and maintain owner/due-date records. | Complaints, safety, accommodations, pay disputes, investigations, and legal questions. |
| `performance-review-administration` | Review cycles contain repeatable roster, deadline, form, and reminder work. | Confirm cycle records, track completion, send approved reminders, and prepare status reports. | Ratings, narrative judgment, performance action, deadline exceptions, and decisions. |
| `offboarding-coordination` | Approved separations require coordinated access, assets, payroll, records, and communications. | Open tasks after authorization, chase system owners, and reconcile closeout evidence. | Separation decision, employee communication, final pay, retention, and legal duties. |

## Strongest services and individual-page decision

Each selected service has an individual page because buyers need to evaluate its tasks, controls, and first-week plan independently. The focused pages avoid presenting broad “HR outsourcing” as permission to perform unrestricted HR work. Related services may share navigation and visual language, but their task lists, stop rules, access needs, and owner responsibilities must remain service-specific.

## Quality and claim-safety rules

- Describe administrative capability, not guaranteed business outcomes.
- Do not publish invented customer quotes, star ratings, savings, placement counts, or success percentages.
- Use first-party or properly licensed illustrations that depict the workflow; avoid decorative stock photography that implies an actual team or client.
- Keep sensitive personal data in the client's approved system of record and expose only the fields needed for the assigned task.
- Require a named client-side reviewer and an escalation path before expanding scope.
- Revisit this record when a service, system, risk boundary, or client promise changes.
